            How Docket works

            Docket is your daily record of work done. No account, nothing sent anywhere — everything saves automatically, as you type, on this device. Not sure what a button does? Hover it — or Tab to it — and it explains itself.

            ● The daily log

            • Type what you did in the box and press Enter — it's saved instantly.
            • The tiles above the log count the open day, its week and its month — click This week or This month to open that view. The small bars chart the last 14 days; click a bar to jump to that day.
            • Move between days with the ‹ › arrows, or click the big date to open a calendar.
            • Hover an entry and click the ✎ pencil to edit it (Save/Cancel), or ✕ to delete it — in the Day, Week and Job views alike.
            • Reorder a day by dragging an entry up or down — in the Day, Week or Job view. Or Tab to its handle (the number, or the coloured dot) and press ↑ ↓. On a touch screen, use the ▲ ▼ buttons beside each entry.
            • In the Week view, the + on a day's card adds an entry to that day without leaving the week.
            • Logged it on the wrong day? Change the date in the editor, or drag the entry onto another day — in Recent days, a Week card or a Month cell. Undo pops up either way.
            • Deleted the wrong thing? An Undo button pops up for a few seconds — same for Clear this day.
            • Recent Days in the sidebar lists every day that has entries, grouped by month — click a month name to fold or unfold it, and the ▦ icon opens that month in the calendar.

            ● Jobs — group work under one heading

            • Doing several things for the same client, site or project? Type its name in the Job box left of the entry box (or pick one from its list) — every entry you add goes against that job.
            • The job stays set, so you can log a run of entries in a row. Click its ✕ (or empty the box) to stop.
            • Entries show their job as a small pill — click the pill, or the job in the sidebar's Jobs list, to open the Job view: every entry on that job, across every day, with totals.
            • In the Job view, Copy Job gives you the job name plus each day's work — ready for an invoice.
            • Wishlist and recurring items take a job too — the Job box appears under their add box when you click into it, and their ✎ pencil changes it. Logging one on a day carries its job onto the entry.
            • The Job view lists the job's wishlist and recurring items above its logged work — click ↳ to log one on the open day.
            • Drag an entry, wishlist or recurring item onto a job in the sidebar (or onto an open Job view) to link it to that job.
            • Every job has its own colour — on its pills, down the edge of its entries, and on the calendar. Click the coloured dot beside a job in the sidebar to change it.
            • The Month calendar shades each day in its busiest job's colour — deeper the busier the day — names the day's jobs, and shows a bar of how the day split between them.
            • In the Jobs list, the thin bar under each job shows its share of the work, measured against your busiest job.
            • Change an entry's job with the ✎ pencil. Rename or delete a job from the sidebar; deleting a job keeps everything that was on it.
            • Finished a job? Hover it and click the archive box. It leaves the Jobs list and the Job box's suggestions, but everything on it stays — Show archived under the list brings it back into view, and Restore returns it. Logging new work against an archived job restores it by itself.

            ● Copy & paste

            • Copy Day to Clipboard copies the date plus a bulleted list — ready to paste into an email, timesheet or invoice.
            • Copy Items Only copies just the lines, no date heading.

            ● Print & PDF reports

            • Print / PDF (or Ctrl P) prints whatever is open as a tidy report in Docket's colours — the day, week, month or job.
            • Each report has its totals, a By job split showing where the time went, and every entry by day with its job's colour. A job's report ends with what's still to do on it.
            • Choose Save as PDF in the print dialog to keep it or attach it to an email or invoice. Leave Background graphics ticked in the print dialog.
            • Two styles, chosen under Reports: Glass is Docket's dark look, best saved as a PDF; Paper is white and ink-friendly, best for a printer. Docket remembers your choice.

            ● Reports — for the paperwork

            • Reports (or R) pulls out any stretch of the log: pick Last week, Last month, This year, or your own From and To dates.
            • Choose All jobs, one job (archived ones too), or Entries with no job. The dialog counts what you'll get before you commit.
            • Then Print / PDF it, Copy it as text for an email, or save Excel (CSV) — one row per entry with its date, day, task and job, ready to sort, filter or total.
            • Reports never change the log. (For a full backup you can import again, use Export JSON or Export CSV in the side panel.)

            ● Paste a whole list

            • Copy several lines from an email, a notes app or a spreadsheet column and paste them into the empty entry box: each line becomes its own entry on the open day, on the job in the Job box.
            • Bullets and numbering (- • 1.) are dropped. It works in the Wishlist and Recurring boxes too, and Undo takes the whole paste back.

            ● Day · Week · Month views

            • Switch views with the Day / Week / Month tabs. The ‹ › arrows then step by day, week or month.
            • Week shows the whole week's entries at a glance, with empty days folded to a slim row (their + still adds to them); Month is a calendar with counts per day.
            • Click any day in either view to open it. The copy buttons follow the view — in Month view, Copy Month gives you the full month-to-date list, ready to paste.

            ● Keyboard shortcuts

            They work whenever you're not typing in a box — press Esc to leave one. Using Tab? The first stop is Skip to the log, straight past the side panel.

            • / or Ctrl K — search every entry, job, wishlist and recurring item; ↑ ↓ then Enter jumps to it
            • Ctrl Z — undo the last delete, move, link, archive, skip, import or clear, for a minute after it happens
            • ← → — previous / next day (steps by week or month in those views)
            • T — jump to today  ·  D W M — switch to Day, Week or Month view
            • N — start typing a new entry  ·  J — pick the job for new entries
            • C — copy the open day, week, month or job  ·  Ctrl P — print the open view as a report
            • ? — open this guide  ·  Esc — close it

            ● Wishlist

            • Queue things that still need doing in the Wishlist — the panel on the right (on smaller screens, under the log).
            • When one gets done, drag it onto the log or onto a day — in Recent Days, or straight onto a Week or Month cell. It becomes an entry there.
            • Or hover it and click ↳ to log it on the day you have open. Click its text or ✎ to edit it or set its job, ✕ to remove.

            ● Recurring tasks

            • Add tasks you do again and again to Recurring in the sidebar.
            • Drag one onto any day (or click ↳ for the open day) to log it — it stays in the list so you can use it next time.
            • If it's already on that day (same job), Docket skips the duplicate and tells you.
            • Due on set days? Click its ✎ and tick the days (say, F for a Friday timesheet). On those days it appears under Due today at the top of today's log: Log adds it, ✕ skips it until next time.

            ● CSV import / export (Excel)

            Bulk-load the month to date from a spreadsheet. One entry per row: date first, task second, and optionally a job third. Put Wishlist or Recurring in the date column to load those lists instead; a fourth Repeats column gives a recurring task its days.

            Date,Task,Job,Repeats
2026-07-01,"Installed blinds, lounge",Smith House
02/07/2026,Quoted new office fit-out,
Wishlist,Order paint,Smith House
Recurring,Timesheet,,Every Friday
Archived,,Garden shed
            • The header row is optional — but to import jobs, include it with Job as the third heading. Dates can be 2026-07-01 or 01/07/2026 style (day-first).
            • Comma, semicolon or tab delimited — detected automatically.
            • Re-importing is safe: duplicates and unreadable rows are skipped and reported. The same task on two different jobs isn't a duplicate. Changed your mind? Every import has an Undo.
            • Export CSV writes the same format, so you can export → edit in Excel → import back. Text that Excel would read as a formula (starting with = + - or @) gets an apostrophe in front, and loses it again on import.

            ● Backups & where your data lives

            • Data is stored by this browser, on this computer. Stick to one browser (e.g. always Edge).
            • Export JSON saves everything — including jobs and the wishlist — to a file; Import JSON merges it back in. Use it to move to another browser or PC, or as a safety net.
            • Clearing the browser's site data erases the log — keep an occasional export. Under Backup & data Docket shows when you last exported, and reminds you when it's been two weeks and the log has changed since.
            • Back up automatically to a folder (Chrome and Edge on a computer): pick a folder once — a OneDrive or Dropbox folder gives you an off-computer copy — and Docket saves a backup there after every change, one file per day, keeping the last 30. If the browser asks for permission again later, click Resume auto-backup.
            • Open in two tabs? They stay in step — a change in one appears in the other.
            • If the browser blocks storage, Docket still works but shows Not saved — export before you close it.
            • Clear data… wipes the log, jobs, wishlist and/or recurring tasks — you tick which. Use it to start fresh, e.g. clear everything then Import JSON to reload a backup cleanly. It offers to export a backup first, and an Undo pops up afterwards.
            • Both JSON and CSV hold everything — the log, jobs (archived or not), wishlist and recurring tasks with their days, each with its job. JSON is the exact backup; CSV is the one to open in Excel.

            ● Install it as an app

            • Opened from the web, Docket can be installed: click Install app (top right), or the install icon in Edge's or Chrome's address bar. It gets its own window and Start menu / dock icon, and works offline.
            • Prefer a file? The single-file version runs by double-clicking — no install, no internet.
            • Moving between the two? Each keeps its own data. Export JSON in the one you're leaving, then Import JSON in the other.

            ● Docket at a glance

            • The ✦ button (top right) opens a short overview of everything Docket does, and what's planned next. Download PDF saves it to keep, print or send to someone.
