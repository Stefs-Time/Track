            How Docket works

            Docket is your daily record of work done, in a single file. No installs, no server, no account — everything lives in this HTML file and saves automatically on your machine. Not sure what a button does? Hover it — or Tab to it — and it explains itself.

            ● The daily log

            • Type what you did in the box and press Enter — it's saved instantly.
            • The tiles above the log count the open day, its week and its month; the small bars chart the last 14 days — click a bar to jump to that day.
            • Move between days with the ‹ › arrows, or click the big date to open a calendar.
            • Hover an entry and click the ✎ pencil to edit it (Save/Cancel), or ✕ to delete it.
            • Deleted the wrong thing? An Undo button pops up for a few seconds — same for Clear this day.
            • Recent Days in the sidebar lists every day that has entries, grouped by month — click a month name to fold or unfold it, and the ▦ icon opens that month in the calendar.

            ● Jobs — group work under one heading

            • Doing several things for the same client, site or project? Type its name in the Job box left of the entry box (or pick one from its list) — every entry you add goes against that job.
            • The job stays set, so you can log a run of entries in a row. Click its ✕ (or empty the box) to stop.
            • Entries show their job as a small pill — click the pill, or the job in the sidebar's Jobs list, to open the Job view: every entry on that job, across every day, with totals.
            • In the Job view, Copy Job gives you the job name plus each day's work — ready for an invoice.
            • Wishlist and recurring items take a job too — the Job box appears under their add box when you click into it, and their ✎ pencil changes it. Logging one on a day carries its job onto the entry.
            • The Job view lists the job's wishlist and recurring items above its logged work — click ↳ to log one on the open day.
            • Change an entry's job with the ✎ pencil. Rename or delete a job from the sidebar; deleting a job keeps everything that was on it.

            ● Copy & paste

            • Copy Day to Clipboard copies the date plus a bulleted list — ready to paste into an email, timesheet or invoice.
            • Copy Items Only copies just the lines, no date heading.

            ● Day · Week · Month views

            • Switch views with the Day / Week / Month tabs. The ‹ › arrows then step by day, week or month.
            • Week shows the whole week's entries at a glance; Month is a calendar with counts per day.
            • Click any day in either view to open it. The copy buttons follow the view — in Month view, Copy Month gives you the full month-to-date list, ready to paste.

            ● Keyboard shortcuts

            They work whenever you're not typing in a box:

            • ← → — previous / next day (steps by week or month in those views)
            • T — jump to today  ·  D W M — switch to Day, Week or Month view
            • N — start typing a new entry  ·  J — pick the job for new entries
            • C — copy the open day, week, month or job
            • ? — open this guide  ·  Esc — close it

            ● Wishlist

            • Queue things that still need doing in the sidebar's Wishlist.
            • When one gets done, drag it onto the log or onto a day — in Recent Days, or straight onto a Week or Month cell. It becomes an entry there.
            • Or hover it and click ↳ to log it on the day you have open. Click its text or ✎ to edit it or set its job, ✕ to remove.

            ● Recurring tasks

            • Add tasks you do again and again to Recurring in the sidebar.
            • Drag one onto any day (or click ↳ for the open day) to log it — it stays in the list so you can use it next time.
            • If it's already on that day (same job), Docket skips the duplicate and tells you.

            ● CSV import / export (Excel)

            Bulk-load the month to date from a spreadsheet. One entry per row: date first, task second, and optionally a job third. Put Wishlist or Recurring in the date column to load those lists instead.

            Date,Task,Job
2026-07-01,"Installed blinds, lounge",Smith House
02/07/2026,Quoted new office fit-out,
Wishlist,Order paint,Smith House
Recurring,Timesheet,
            • The header row is optional — but to import jobs, include it with Job as the third heading. Dates can be 2026-07-01 or 01/07/2026 style (day-first).
            • Comma, semicolon or tab delimited — detected automatically.
            • Re-importing is safe: duplicates and unreadable rows are skipped and reported. The same task on two different jobs isn't a duplicate.
            • Export CSV writes the same format, so you can export → edit in Excel → import back.

            ● Backups & where your data lives

            • Data is stored by this browser, on this computer. Stick to one browser (e.g. always Edge).
            • Export JSON saves everything — including jobs and the wishlist — to a file; Import JSON merges it back in. Use it to move to another browser or PC, or as a safety net.
            • Clearing the browser's site data erases the log — keep an occasional export.
            • Clear data… wipes the log, jobs, wishlist and/or recurring tasks — you tick which. Use it to start fresh, e.g. clear everything then Import JSON to reload a backup cleanly. It offers to export a backup first, and an Undo pops up afterwards.
            • Both JSON and CSV hold everything — the log, jobs, wishlist and recurring tasks, each with its job. JSON is the exact backup; CSV is the one to open in Excel.
            Tip: right-click the Docket.html file → Send to → Desktop (create shortcut) so it's always one click away. Reopen this guide any time with the ? button, top right.
